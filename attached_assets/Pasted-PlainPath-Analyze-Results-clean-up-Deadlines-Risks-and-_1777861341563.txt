PlainPath Analyze Results — clean up Deadlines, Risks, and overlap between tabs

Goal:
Improve the Analyze results page information hierarchy so each tab has a clear purpose and the page feels more premium, less repetitive, and easier to trust.

Do not redesign the whole app.
Do not change billing.
Do not change RevenueCat.
Do not reintroduce Ask This Document.
Do not add higher-tier messaging.
Do not change the analysis engine unless needed to map fields correctly.
Focus on presentation, grouping, labels, and clarity.

Launch scope:
- Analyze a Document
- Contract Review
- PlainPath Pro only

--------------------------------------------------
1. DEADLINES TAB SHOULD FOCUS ONLY ON DATE/TIME OBLIGATIONS
--------------------------------------------------

Current issue:
The Deadlines page mixes hard deadlines, watch dates, contract terms, payment timing, and general action items. It feels less clear than before.

Update the Deadlines tab so it is organized into clear deadline-specific groups:

A. Hard Deadlines — Do Not Miss
Use for:
- appeal windows
- payment due dates
- submission windows
- cancellation notice windows
- cure periods
- response deadlines
- anything where missing the date causes loss of rights, fees, denial, termination, or invalidation

Color:
Red only.

B. Watch Dates — Review Carefully
Use for:
- effective dates
- installment dates
- renewal dates
- scheduled milestones
- review dates
- dates that matter but are not necessarily urgent

Color:
Amber/orange.

C. Reference Dates — For Context
Use for:
- contract term length
- survival periods
- general background timing
- dates that explain the document but do not require immediate action

Color:
Blue/neutral.

Do not place all date-related items under red.

--------------------------------------------------
2. EACH DEADLINE CARD SHOULD EXPLAIN THE DEADLINE CLEARLY
--------------------------------------------------

Each deadline card should show:

- Deadline / date trigger
- What it means
- What the user must do
- What happens if missed
- Source quote / evidence line
- Confidence badge if available

Suggested card layout:

Title:
Invoice payment due date

Trigger:
Within fifteen (15) calendar days of each invoice date

Plain English:
You must pay each invoice within 15 calendar days after it is issued.

Why it matters:
Late payment may trigger interest, service delay, or dispute issues.

Source:
“Payment is due within fifteen (15) calendar days of invoice date…”

Do not make the user infer the consequence.

--------------------------------------------------
3. FIX DEADLINE COUNT LABELING
--------------------------------------------------

Current issue:
Top chips and tab counts can confuse hard deadlines with watch dates or risk scores.

Rules:
- Deadlines tab count should represent actual date/timing items.
- Inside the Deadlines page, show separate counts:
  - Hard Deadlines: X
  - Watch Dates: X
  - Reference Dates: X

If the top chip says “5 Deadlines,” the page should clearly explain whether that means:
- 5 hard deadlines only
or
- 5 total deadline/date items

Preferred:
Top chip:
“5 Deadlines”

Inside page:
“5 hard deadlines”
“3 watch dates”

No confusing mismatch.

--------------------------------------------------
4. REDUCE OVERLAP BETWEEN OVERVIEW, WHAT’S MISSING, DEADLINES, RISKS, AND ACTION PACK
--------------------------------------------------

Each tab should have a distinct job:

Overview:
- Show a short executive snapshot
- Show top 3–5 urgent items only
- Do not duplicate entire Deadlines/Risks/What’s Missing lists

What’s Missing:
- Only missing info, missing documents, missing clauses, missing user input
- Do not show normal deadlines here unless a missing deadline date makes the document incomplete

Deadlines:
- Only dates, timing windows, payment windows, notice windows, expiration/renewal/term timing

Risks & Notes:
- Only risk explanations and potential consequences

Action Pack:
- Only recommended next steps/checklist items

If an item appears in multiple places, summarize it in Overview but show the full explanation only in the correct dedicated tab.

--------------------------------------------------
5. CLEAN COLOR SEVERITY RULES GLOBALLY
--------------------------------------------------

Apply consistent severity colors across all result tabs:

Critical / High:
- red
- use only when there is serious consequence or immediate action needed

Medium / Review Carefully:
- amber/orange
- use for important but not immediate issues

Low / Informational:
- blue/gray
- use for context or neutral notices

Confirmed / Positive:
- green

Missing / Needs Input:
- blue or purple
- do not use red unless the missing item blocks submission, payment, appeal, filing, or contract execution

Do not overuse red.
Red should mean “pay attention now.”

--------------------------------------------------
6. IMPROVE RISKS & NOTES EXPLANATIONS
--------------------------------------------------

Risk cards should be more explanatory.

Each risk card should include:

- Risk title
- Why this is risky
- What could happen
- What document language triggered it
- What to verify or do next
- Source quote / evidence

Example structure:

Risk:
Limited remedies due to liability cap and damages exclusion

Why this matters:
If something goes wrong, the provider may limit what you can recover.

Potential impact:
You may not be able to recover indirect damages or losses beyond the fees paid.

Source:
“Neither party shall be liable for indirect, incidental, consequential…”

Suggested next step:
Review whether this limitation is acceptable before signing.

Do not use vague one-line risk explanations when source-backed detail exists.
