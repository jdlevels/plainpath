Please refine the canvas with the following requirements:

1. Make the Completed Contract Review screen feel like a decision dashboard, not a legal memo.

The completed screen should default to the most important items first:
A. Plain-English Contract Summary
B. Risk / Confidence Strip
C. Key Contract Risks
D. Required Next Steps
E. Source Traceability

Then show deeper sections in compact/collapsible format:
- Obligations & Responsibilities
- Payment / Fees / Penalties
- Termination / Renewal
- Missing or Weak Protections

Do not show every contract detail expanded by default.

2. Tighten the Plain-English Contract Summary.

Keep it to 4–6 lines maximum.

It should answer:
- What type of contract is this?
- Who are the parties?
- What does the user need to pay attention to?
- What is the biggest risk or action item?

Do not make it sound like legal advice.

3. Strengthen the legal disclaimer language without overloading the UI.

Use careful wording:
- “Contract review support”
- “Risk indicators”
- “Terms to verify”
- “Possible missing protections”
- “Review with a qualified professional if high-risk”
- “Not legal advice”

Avoid:
- “This contract is legally invalid”
- “You should sign”
- “You should not sign”
- “This guarantees protection”
- “This replaces an attorney”

4. Refine the Key Contract Risks section.

Show only the top 3–5 risks by default.

Each risk card should include:
- short risk title
- one-line explanation
- severity chip
- source chip
- recommended action

Use red only for truly critical/high-risk clauses.
Use amber for caution.
Use purple for source traceability.

5. Refine Required Next Steps.

Make the checklist practical and action-focused:
- verify parties and dates
- confirm payment terms
- review termination clause
- check renewal or auto-renewal language
- confirm dispute resolution terms
- confirm missing exhibits/attachments
- review with a qualified professional if high-risk

Keep this section near the top because it tells the user what to do.

6. Refine Obligations & Responsibilities.

Use a compact table/card hybrid:
- obligation
- responsible party
- deadline/date if available
- source chip

Do not make this a long paragraph section.

7. Refine Payment / Fees / Penalties.

Show compact items:
- amount
- due date
- recurring fee
- late fee
- penalty
- refund/cancellation term
- source chip

8. Refine Termination / Renewal.

Show compact items:
- termination right
- notice period
- auto-renewal
- early termination penalty
- renewal deadline
- source chip

9. Refine Missing or Weak Protections.

Use cautious language:
- “Possible missing protection”
- “Term may be unclear”
- “Verify whether this should be included”
- “Consider professional review”

Examples:
- unclear termination right
- missing liability cap
- unclear dispute resolution
- missing signatures
- missing attachments/exhibits
- vague performance standard
- unclear refund or cancellation terms

10. Preserve source chip behavior.

When a source chip is clicked:
- source chip becomes active
- matching clause in the left document viewer highlights
- document scrolls to the matching section/page
- evidence banner shows the matched text
- related contract finding remains active on the right panel
