Additional requirement — clause-level “Questions to Ask” guidance

Add a practical “Questions to Ask” section to each Key Clause card and each Risk card where appropriate.

Purpose:
Help the user know what to ask, clarify, or negotiate before signing if a clause appears one-sided, unclear, unfair, missing, suspicious, or risky.

Do not call this section “Rebuttal.”
Use user-facing language:

“Questions to Ask”

or, if space allows:

“Questions / Changes to Ask For”

Each clause card should show:

- Clause name
- What it says
- Why it matters
- What to check before signing
- Questions to Ask
- Source quote or clause reference
- Risk level

Questions to Ask should be written as plain-English prompts the user can copy or say to the other party.

Examples:

Payment clause:
- “When exactly is payment due?”
- “Are there late fees?”
- “What happens if the work is delayed or incomplete?”

Termination clause:
- “Can either side terminate early?”
- “Is written notice required?”
- “Are there fees or penalties for ending the agreement?”

Liability clause:
- “Can this liability cap be increased?”
- “Are there exceptions for confidentiality breaches, fraud, or gross negligence?”
- “Does this limit apply even if the other party causes major loss?”

Intellectual property clause:
- “Who owns the final work after payment?”
- “Do I receive full ownership or only a license?”
- “Can the provider reuse the work elsewhere?”

Confidentiality clause:
- “How long does confidentiality last?”
- “What information is excluded?”
- “What happens if confidential information is disclosed?”

Missing exhibit/schedule:
- “Can you provide the missing exhibit before signing?”
- “Is this attachment part of the agreement?”
- “Does the contract still apply if the exhibit is missing?”

Rules:
- Do not tell the user what to decide.
- Do not say “do not sign.”
- Do not provide legal conclusions.
- Do not use aggressive or adversarial language.
- Use “ask,” “clarify,” “confirm,” and “consider requesting.”
- Keep questions short and practical.
- If the contract text does not support a specific question, use a general clarification question only.

Risk cards should also include:
“Questions to Ask Before Signing”

Each risk card should show:
- Risk title
- Why it matters
- Possible consequence
- Questions to Ask Before Signing
- Source quote

Avoid duplicating the exact same questions across every card.
